ABOUT ME

I find it fulfilling to find solutions for intricate problems, and to document them so completely that a project can be understood in all its complexity.

My name is Dávid László Szekeres. I am from Budapest, Hungary, where I am currently studying architecture at MOME, the Moholy-Nagy University of Art and Design. Before that I spent three years studying architecture at Universitat Internacional de Catalunya in Barcelona, Spain. I am always looking for ways to challenge myself professionally. If I had to highlight my best skill, it would be my creativity and my ability to construct complex systems.

Observing and documenting the world around me comes as second nature. I try to learn an extra skill with every project. That way I improve my designs and get to experiment with techniques I have not used before.

What follows is a body of work from university and from time spent interning at different studios that I believe best represents that mentality.

Gardening as a hands-on pursuit: working with soil and plants sharpens my sense of how space, light and material behave beyond the walls of a building.

This garden, conceived and realised collaboratively with a dedicated team, reflects an ongoing dialogue between architecture and nature, offering lessons that inform my design thinking in profound ways.

Central to the design are material circularity and biodiversity: repurposing, reusing and selecting elements that can return to the earth, and choosing species that support pollinators, native wildlife and a resilient, layered ecosystem.

Category

Year

Location

A canoeing club in Zaragoza with exposed brick façades and a complete reinterpretation of its site, giving space back to the native wildlife of the Ebro.

The Hispalyt Ceramic Forum competition challenges students and recent graduates to design a project in which ceramic materials, particularly exposed brick, are fundamental.

My aim was to rehabilitate the site through sustainable, cost-effective practice, creating a new environment for the river’s ecosystem in tandem with the building’s intended function.

SITE PROBLEMS

Four problems,
four responses.

Large concrete pier

The primary source of problems: unnatural, unappealing and underutilised. Demolition is a reasonable solution.

Barren surroundings

The path intersection is a massive concrete volume, making renaturalisation difficult. Remodelling is suitable.

Unstable shore

Periodic flooding of the Ebro poses structural risk below street level. An adaptive structure is required.

Unappealing building

The existing club building sits in disrepair. Refurbishment and repurposing are crucial.

PROPOSED SOLUTIONS

ADemolition

Removing the massive concrete pier opens spatial opportunities and produces crucial building material.

BReutilisation

The broken-up concrete fills corten-steel gabion baskets that stabilise the new shoreline.

CRenaturalisation

Common reed and weeping willows replace the pier, creating new habitat and natural shore stabilisation.

D0 km materials

Easily sourced materials, partly salvaged from the demolition, a mentality at the forefront of the project.

GRID
808

Gantry Rigged Industrial Development

Category
Art / Architecture
Tools
Watercolours, Rhino 3D, Grasshopper + Wasp, Photoshop, Illustrator
Year
2025
Movement
Russian Constructivism

The task was to interpret a 20th-century art movement through an architectural project. Russian Constructivism, with its focus on structure, order and the social role of design, became GRID 808: modular composition and material clarity in a contemporary framework.

Here, humanity is obsolete. The machine has replaced the organism. The structure has replaced the soul. The G.R.I.D. is not alive. It simply persists.

01Concept

My first drawing examined scale contrasts so extreme they blurred the boundary between structure, object and landscape.

02Focal points

Inspired by the Halo series, “towers” became the sole connection between the floating landscape of machinery and the Earth.

03Components

Five modules, organised in an organised chaos through the Grasshopper plugin Wasp.

G.R.I.D. COMPONENT LIBRARY05 MODULES

Control towerThe vertical anchor, the only link between the machine landscape and the ground.

Connective unitThe main fibre of the GRID: lets product flow between units, creating a complex maze of connections.

Storage unitMassive industrial storage, holding the product before and after refinement.

Branch unitDiverts the flow of product in the G.R.I.D. to different sectors.

Ventilation unitProtruding from the control towers, dissipating the heat of production.
